Formal Methods Summer School: an Interactive Study Companion

Interactive notes for the lectures of the Marktoberdorf summer school, built from the speakers' slide decks and from recordings of the talks. Play with the widgets, step through the algorithms, and use the quizzes to check yourself. Every section can be marked as read, and you can highlight any passage you want to come back to.

About this page. A personal study companion for the Marktoberdorf Summer School of August 2026. It was put together from the lectures and their materials, and it is not an official publication of the school.

Acknowledgements. The sections on deductive verification follow the lectures and the slide deck The Why and How of Deductive Program Verification by Jean-Christophe Filliâtre (CNRS). The section on separation logic follows the lecture and the deck Automating Separation Logic Proofs with Viper by Peter Müller (ETH Zurich). The sections on constrained Horn clauses follow the lectures and the decks Constrained Horn Clauses and their Applications by Orna Grumberg (Technion), including joint work with Omer Rappoport, Yakir Vizel, Bat-chen Rothenberg, Eytan Singher and Mishel Carelli. The sections on AI systems follow the lectures and the deck Evaluation and Security of AI-Based Systems by John C. Mitchell (Stanford University). The sections on testing follow the lectures and the decks Integration Testing and Scenario-Based Tests for Cyber-Physical Systems by Alexander Pretschner (TUM, fortiss and bidt), which report joint work with Michael Wolf, Florian Hauer, Nicola Kolb, Tabea Schmidt and Thomas Hutzelmann. The section on mechanized language semantics follows the lecture and the deck Mechanized Specifications Adopted by Real-World PLs by Sukyoung Ryu (KAIST), reporting joint work with Jihyeok Park and colleagues. The sections on saturation, superposition and lambda-superposition follow the lectures and the decks of Jasmin Blanchette (LMU Munich); his fourth lecture had no deck and is reconstructed from the recording alone. The sections on linearity and on verifying Rust follow the lectures and the deck Linearity, Rust and Verification by Jonathan Protzenko (Google), reporting joint work with Son Ho, Guillaume Boisseau, Aymeric Fromherz and the Aeneas collaborators. The sections on Lean follow the lectures and the decks of Leonardo de Moura; his introductory deck was circulated only as page images, so the first of those sections cites no slides. The sections on proof in industry follow the lectures and the deck Doing Proof: Today, Tomorrow, Next Year by Mike Dodds. The notes cover the lectures only. The school's discussion sessions, the questions asked from the floor, and any conversation caught on the same recordings are deliberately left out, and no student or attendee is named or quoted. Where a name appears it is a lecturer, a published author of work being cited, or a figure from the history of the subject. All slide images remain © their authors and are reproduced with attribution for private study only. Warm thanks to all the lecturers and to the school organizers.
